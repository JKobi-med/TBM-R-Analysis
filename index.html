Our study is based on the TBM study (1), a two-site, longitudinal, randomized, activly-controlled, (“open-label” or unblined) intervention study, in which retired healthy older adults, received prcatical piano training once a week (intervention) or theoretical music lesseons (active control) for 18? Blood samples were taken at three different time points. The first (T0) one week before the weekly cycle of practical or theoretical music lessons were started. The second (T1) after 6 months and the third (T2) again after 6 months so one year after T0.

Because multiple tests were performed on each inidivuals time point (not only blood samples but also fMRI as part of the original study) T0 fell between March and June 2019, T1 between August 2019 and February 2020 and T2 between June 2020 and November 2020.

2.4 Measurement of DNA methylation

2.4.1 Target Genes

We focused on the primer regions of three genes commonly neuroplasticity-associated and hypthecised being potentially epigenetically modifiable by playing the piano: BDNF4, PSD95, MAPT (Tau).

2.4.2 DNA isolation

Genomic DNA (gDNA) was isolated at the Institute of Molecular and Translational Therapeutic Strategies of Hannover Medical School from 50 µL blood using the “NucleoMag Blood 200 µl” kit (MACHEREY-NAGEL GmbH & Co. KG, Düren, Deutschland) in concordance with the instructions of the manufacturer. The isolated gDNA was stored at -20 °C. The blood samples have been received anonymized and prior to gDNA isolation. Then the samples were additionaly randomized (by doing what?) to minimize potential batch effects. DNA samples were diluted in 96-well plates to a fixed concentration of 10 ng/µl.

2.4.3 Bisulfite Conversion

The gDNA of all samples was then bisullfite converted via the “EZ-96 DNA Methylation-Lightning™ MagPrep” kit (Zymo Research Corporation, Irvine, CA, USA) also following the manufacturers instructions.

2.4.4 Primer design

With Ensembl and Geneious Pro 5.6.7 the primer regions of our target genes were located and the sequences of the primer regions analyzed. Aided by Biocalculator (metabion), NetPrimer (Premier Biosoft), Methyl Primer Express v1.0 (Applied Biosystems) and Bisulfite Primer Seeker (Zymo) we designed primers for PCR with bisulfite-converted DNA (see Table 1) and sequencing primers (Table 2) - later used to prepare Sanger Sequencing - and orderd these primers from metabion GmbH (Martinsried, Deutschland).

Note: Other sequencing primers are missing.

2.4.5 Gene Amplification

The primers were dissolved in nuclease-free water. The proportions have been predetermined by the manufacturer. The stock solution 100 pmol. It was then used to derive a working solution of 20 pmol (1:5). Both solutions were prexserved atg -20 °C. Amplification was then performed together with HotStarTaq® PCR Master Mix Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Deutschland). The samples were then puriefed with the PCR purification kit “CleanNGS” (CleanNA, Waddinxveen, Niederlande).

2.4.6 Sanger Sequencing

The purfied PCR prodcus were sequenced with the BigDye Terminator v3.1 Cycle Sequencing Kit (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) and th e sequencing primers mentioned above (Table 2). The sequencing product were purifed with the “CleanDTR” kit (CleanNA, Waddinxveen, Niederlande).

Then using the Applied Biosystems’ 3500 Genetic Analyzer (ABI 3500xL) Capillary electrophoresis was performed.

2.4.7 Data Processing via ESME

The data derived from the ABI 3500xL was then processed with Epigenetic Sequencing Methylation (ESME) analysis software.

3.1 Descriptive statistics

3.1.1 Generall demographics

Overall 144 (one with missing T0! ID 141) participants were included in the study. Both groups were nearly equally distributed (46,5 % in the piano group). The mean age was 69.58 years (SD: 3.5 years) with a minimum of 62 and a maximum of 78 years. This was due to the desgin of the TBM study.

Chat: Continuous variables were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

3.1.2 Metadata sequencing

1,078 blood samples have been sequenced from the participants for three different genes at three different time points (T0, T1, T2). A decline in the number of samples from T0 to T2 can be seen (Table 4), because not every participant provided blood samples at every time point, because of loss to follow up. This is the reason for the difference between the time points.

Furthermore, not every blood sample could be analyzed successfully (cf. Table 4) due to some qualitative differences in the sequencing process. But in the end, a total of 359 BDNF, 362 Tau and 357 PSD95 samples could be successfully sequenced. This is the reason for the slight difference between genes.

Two further Participants had to be excluded from the analysis, because somehow their methylationvalues for timepoints conflated, so they could not be differentiated anymore.

3.1.3 Methylation Level Analysis

3.1.3.1 Overall and over time

Overall the methlation of the three genes was low (Mean < 0,08, Median < 0,04). PSD95 had the lowest methylation values.

3.1.3.2 Per CpG and Gene

Looking at the methlation patterns of every CpG per gene (Figure 3), Tau showed a methylation pattern, that was largely limited to upstream region of the promoter, whereas further downstream CpGs were rarely methylated across individuals. In contrast, for PSD95, CpGs near the end of the promoter region showed notable methylation patterns, while most other sites where mostly none-methylated with little or no inter-individual variability. For BDNF, a comparably localized pattern was less pronounced but further downsstream the CpGs were also less methylated.

3.2 Principal component analysis (PCA)

We performed principal component analyses (PCA) separately for each gene, using the correlation matrix. Across genes, the first two principal components explained only a moderate proportion of total variance (BDNF: PC1 28.81%, PC2 16.84%; Tau: PC1 29.02%, PC2 9.22%; PSD95: PC1 17.10%, PC2 11.61%). Accordingly, the scree plots (Figure 12) show only a gradual decrease of explained variance across subsequent components. (Stimmt nicht für PSD95 und Tau)

Furthermore, biplots of the first two principal components did not indicate a differing clustering pattern between the two group at any timepoint (Figure 4). Although the apparent group-wise configuration for PSD95 varied somewhat across time points, this pattern did not amount to a consistent clustering difference.

Noticably, inspecting the loadings revelaed that the variance explained by PC1 was driven primarily by a small fraction of CpGs in every gene (Figure 5). For BDNF, m148, m066, m111 and m058 have been the strongest contributors; for Tau, m331, m337, m310, m315 and m323; and for PSD95: “p95”, “p60”, “p23”. By ranking the absolute PC1 loadings every gene showed an “elbow” pattern (BDNF: between the 4th and 5th CpG; Tau: between the 4th and 5th CpG; PSD95: after the 3rd CpG; Figure 5; see also Table 8–Table 10 for loading ranks).

This concentration of variance is also evident in the methylation distributions across CpGs (Figure 3) already mentioned above (#sec-results-methylation-cpg-gene). These observations motivated stratifying subsequent analyses by considering (i) all CpGs and (ii) only CpGs exhibiting higher inter-individual variability.

3.3 Analysis of Methylation differences
